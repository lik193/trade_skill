# Design a trading experiment

Begin with a puzzle in the available data or trading process. Look closely at a surprise, an ignored trade, or a pair of similar events that ended differently. Ask who might urgently need liquidity, inventory relief, insurance or exposure, what the price already reflects, and why the opportunity could last until our order arrives. Label inferred motives as hypotheses. If the pattern is empirical, name the unknown cause.

Choose the simplest instrument that expresses the view. For FX, identify the currency legs, venue, session and financing over the hold. For options, specify the contract, premium and exit horizon. Price the outcome where direction is right and magnitude or timing is wrong. For a swing, locate the proposed return between actual entry and exit, including any overnight gap. Apply the mechanics of the instrument held.

Define the event so another person could select it from fields available at decision time. State the eligible universe, entry, exit and net payoff per unit. Name a competing explanation and a case where predictions diverge. If a move depends on a foreign market opening, what happens when the local clocks shift an hour apart?

Choose a simple tradable comparator. Identify which dates were used to discover or tune the idea and whether untouched history is already available. Freeze the prediction before inspecting that evidence. When asked to test, run the comparison if the inputs permit it. Distinguish evidence for the trade from evidence for its proposed cause. A small uncertain result in either direction can leave the decision open.

Express sizing in the user's risk units. Estimate the loss per unit in a plausible adverse move at an executable price, including existing exposure to the same shock. Show when larger, smaller or zero exposure would make sense within the supplied budget. If inputs are absent, work per unit. Deliver the smallest experiment that could change the decision.
