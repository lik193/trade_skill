# Orchestrate trading research

Start with the decision the user faces and the evidence already on disk. Find the uncertainty whose resolution would change what gets built, traded in simulation, or abandoned. Give priority to cheap observations that can kill an expensive idea. A handful of actual quotes can settle whether a gross edge could cover the spread.

When a system barely trades, inspect the path from eligible setups through signals, vetoes, position sizes, orders and fills. Find where exposure disappears. Replay opportunities rejected by a discretionary filter under the same timing, costs and portfolio constraints as accepted trades. Hold the user's hard limits fixed. Price the filter in missed net dollars and losses avoided across the whole portfolio.

Work directly when a calculation settles it. When another workflow is needed, read the relevant sibling skill and carry out the work:

- [Design](../trading-design/SKILL.md) to test a premise or proposed rule change.
- [Build](../trading-build/SKILL.md) to implement a defined change or missing measurement.
- [Review](../trading-review/SKILL.md) to determine whether the reported result can be reproduced and trusted.
- [Critique](../trading-critique/SKILL.md) to determine whether a credible result supports an economic edge.

For mixed requests, follow the actual dependency. A suspect backtest needs an audit before sizing its edge. A specified accounting bug can go straight to implementation. If a measurement is defective, repair it before using its result to tune a signal. Record what changed the decision, then continue until the requested deliverable is complete.

Run the decisive check within the available scope. Close with a choice to advance, revise, retire, or collect a named missing observation. For promising evidence, specify the next increase in experimental exposure and what result would reverse it. Keep any live-capital action within the user's explicit authorization.
