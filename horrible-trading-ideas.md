# The world's worst trading research desk

A collection of fictional proposals from a desk that has just discovered its own backtest goes back to 1843, including EUR/USD.

## Orchestrating the desk

1. **The Committee for Independent Agreement.** Give 31 analysts the same model, the same prompt, the same price feed and different necktie colors. Call each unanimous vote 31 independent pieces of evidence. When they disagree, sack the purple ties. The desk has discovered how to increase its statistical confidence by buying more ties.
2. **The Department of Tomorrow's Yesterday.** Every morning, select yesterday's most profitable strategy. Credit it with yesterday's P&L as its first day in office. Fire it before today's close so its successor can claim today's best result. Each appointment is fully justified by evidence available at the time of the appointment.
3. **The Risk Officer's Perfect Quarter.** Pay the risk officer for preventing losses and the research director for discovering winners. Give the officer veto power over every order and let research score every unfilled idea at its day's best price. Both collect the maximum bonus. Treasury has 47 cents of interest income to fund it.

## Designing signals

4. **The Reciprocal Exchange-Rate Arbitrage.** The EUR/USD analyst sees an uptrend. The USD/EUR analyst sees a downtrend. Buy EUR/USD and sell USD/EUR. Announce a dispute between two markets and a perfectly diversified book. Then ask why both positions lose when the dollar rallies.
5. **The Treaty of 150.** USD/JPY keeps reacting around 150. To establish whether traders respect round numbers, rewrite the feed in hundredths of a yen. Discover even stronger support at 15,000. Add both features. The same observation has now survived independent replication at a much larger number.
6. **Thermal Momentum.** Train a webcam on the laptop and buy whenever the fan spins up before payrolls. The pilot works beautifully. The historical replay process uses an extra CPU core to render large candles after it reads the next tick. Hire a meteorologist to explain why warm air predicts dollar strength.

## Building the simulator

7. **The Schrödinger Limit Order.** If the low touches our bid and price rallies, record a fill. If it touches and keeps falling, record that we were behind someone in the queue. Both explanations are individually plausible. Choose between them at the close, when the market has supplied better information.
8. **The Infinite Liquidity Family Plan.** A quote offers one contract. Create a child portfolio for each of 10,000 strategies. Each child correctly checks that its one-contract order fits the displayed size. Consolidate the children after the fill. Attribute the resulting capacity to excellent parenting.
9. **The Magic Options Multiplier.** Store purchase premiums per share and sale proceeds per standard 100-share contract. Buying an option quoted at $2 costs the simulator $2. Selling it unchanged brings in $200. The developer hand-checks both prices against the chain and declares the $198 profit fully reconciled.

## Reviewing the evidence

10. **The 9:31 Time Machine.** A simulation downloads the revised 18:00 economic release, then sets its input timestamp to the original 09:30 release time. The auditor verifies that 09:30 precedes the 09:31 order. Every timestamp is in UTC. The report gives the system extra credit for temporal rigor.
11. **The Museum of Extant Companies.** Build a century-long stock-picking history using today's surviving companies. Exhibit the winners in the main gallery. Reserve an equally large room for bankrupt constituents. Leave it empty because the vendor returned no rows.
12. **The Photogenic Holdout.** Seal the test period, forbid all access to its data, and permit the team to receive only one bit of feedback per submission: whether the equity curve looks better. After 40,000 submissions, unveil the winning strategy and the still-intact wax seal.

## Critiquing the economics

13. **The Fire Insurance Exit Interview.** Cancel every hedge that lost money on a day the building stayed upright. Promote the short-volatility strategy that collected a premium on those same days. Charge the next fire to Facilities so the investment committee can evaluate both decisions on a consistent basis.
14. **The Drawdown Calendar Reform.** A strategy takes six months to recover. Convene a committee to redefine a trading day as the interval between successive portfolio highs. Drawdown on end-of-day marks falls to zero. The fund's first difficult question comes from a client trying to withdraw on a Tuesday that officially hasn't ended since March.
15. **The Inverse Oracle.** Hire a forecaster so bad that doing the opposite must be profitable. In the trial, the forecaster loses $2 and its perfect inverse also loses $2. Both round trips crossed the same $2 spread in a flat market. Hire a second philosopher to determine the opposite of a transaction fee.
